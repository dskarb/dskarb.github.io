SlotCatcher

A SaaS that fills cancelled appointments by texting a waiting list. I built all of it, tried to sell it, and no salon paid. This is what I learned.

The idea

A cancelled appointment is lost revenue. Calling or messaging clients by hand takes time, and the slot usually stays empty anyway.

SlotCatcher is for appointment-based businesses: hair and beauty salons, massage, physiotherapy, clinics, tattoo studios. It works next to the calendar they already use and replaces nothing.

How it works

The owner needs about one click after a cancellation.

  • The salon builds a waiting list through a public form, a QR code, a CSV import or by hand. Clients don't create accounts.
  • After a cancellation the owner creates a "rescue": a service and a time.
  • Matching clients get an SMS and/or email with a unique one-time link.
  • The first person to click it gets the slot. The owner and the client both get an email confirmation.

The parts that were hard

The product looks simple, but a few places had to be correct under concurrency, because they involve money and double bookings.

  • First come, first served without races: claiming a slot is one atomic database function (claim_rescue), so two clients clicking at the same moment can never both win.
  • SMS limits enforced atomically: a database function checks the monthly quota while it creates the tokens, so concurrent sends can't overshoot it, and a send can go partially through within the remaining limit.
  • SMS cost: Polish characters are stripped so a message fits in 160 GSM-7 characters instead of 70, and links are shortened.
  • Billing: Stripe subscriptions in PLN and EUR with a 14-day trial. Plan limits live in Stripe price metadata and are synced by a webhook with signature checks and idempotency.

Security and structure

PostgreSQL row-level security (RLS) on every table, with the Supabase service-role key used only on the server. Next.js middleware handles auth, CSRF protection (Origin check) and a per-request CSP nonce. Claim tokens are 32-character one-time values validated with Zod, and claim attempts are rate limited.

The interface is available in Polish and English, and notifications go out in seven languages depending on the client's preference. Built on the Next.js App Router with Supabase Auth and SQL migrations, and covered by Vitest tests.

Trying to sell it

I built a landing page with a loss calculator, pricing and FAQ, tuned for traffic from Meta Ads, and tested demand with cold emails to salons and Instagram ads. No salon paid.

What I'd change

The product worked. The mistake was the order of the work: I wrote the code first and talked to customers second.

  • Talk to salon owners before writing any code, and ask how they deal with cancellations today.
  • Check that someone will pay using a manual version first, before building the automation.
  • Treat the landing page and ads as an experiment about demand, not as a launch.

Why I'm showing it

It ended without revenue, and it taught me more about product work than most projects that shipped. I'd rather show that honestly than hide it.
