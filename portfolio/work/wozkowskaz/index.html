Wózkowskaz

Maps tell you how to get there, not whether a stroller will make it. Wózkowskaz gives parents a plain verdict before they leave the house.

The problem

A parent planning a walk can't tell in advance whether the route has stairs, mud, gravel or a steep climb. They find out on the spot, with a child in the stroller.

Ordinary maps solve a different problem. They find a way from A to B, and say nothing about whether a wheeled stroller can use it.

One verdict, with reasons

The output is a single answer without GIS jargon, always shown with the reasons behind it:

  • City stroller: mostly smooth surface, no stairs, gentle slopes.
  • Off-road stroller: gravel or packed earth, or longer climbs. Needs big, air-filled wheels.
  • Carrier only: stairs, long unpaved stretches or very steep sections.

What it does

The verdict works at three scales.

  • Area scan: zoom in and the app colors every path by surface (smooth, off-road, unpaved, unknown), marks obstacles such as stairs and barriers, and summarizes the area in a card.
  • A → B route: pick two points, get a walking route and a full report with distance, time, elevation profile, surface shares and warnings on the map.
  • Nap walk: choose a start and 30, 45 or 60 minutes, and the app proposes up to three loops, ranked by verdict, fit to the time, flatness, and benches and playgrounds along the way.
  • Also: rain forecast for the walk time, parent-relevant places (benches, shelters, toilets, playgrounds), shareable URLs, PWA, PL/EN and a dark theme.

Data and routing

Everything comes from free, keyless sources, so the product can stay free, with no accounts and no database.

  • Surface, stairs and places: OpenStreetMap, via the OSM API and Overpass mirrors.
  • Walking routes: BRouter, with OSRM as a fallback.
  • Elevation: GPX/BRouter data first, then EU-DEM 25 m, then Copernicus 90 m via Open-Meteo.
  • Place search: Nominatim. Map tiles: OpenFreeMap.

Engineering decisions

The interesting problem is reliability. The app depends on public APIs that have rate limits and sometimes go down, and it is used outdoors on a phone.

  • Gaps in OSM data lower the confidence of a verdict but never change its result. Shares are computed only over surfaces with a known value.
  • Two-layer cache: an in-memory LRU plus optional Upstash Redis, keyed per map tile, with single-flight request deduplication to avoid duplicate upstream calls and CDN cache headers on top.
  • Report cache keys include an analyzer version, so a change to the verdict logic can never serve stale results.
  • Graceful failure: Overpass mirrors, routing and elevation fallbacks, an explicit incomplete flag on partial scans, and a readable error instead of an empty result when a source is down.
  • Stateless by design. Analytics events carry no coordinates or place names.
  • Unit tests (Vitest) cover the analyzer, verdict thresholds, OSM matching, elevation, GPX, cache, tiles, loops and the API routes.

Honest limits

The data is indicative. OpenStreetMap is incomplete in places, and the product says so: on site, the parent's own judgment decides.

Where it's going

The direction is a shift from "check a route" to "where should we walk today so it goes well": nap loops, weather, places. Next on the roadmap are stroller-aware routing, an equipment profile and community confirmations. The north star metric is the number of walks planned per week by returning parents.
